# Dojo Deckbuilder · v2.3 Final Report

## Honest assessment

Dojo Deckbuilder now has an identifiable game inside the joke. H.I.Y.A.H., zones, visible Combo goals, and Belt tasks form a genuinely distinctive skeleton, while the Paper-Fu voice is consistent enough to feel authored rather than assembled from generic fantasy copy.

The remaining weakness is foundational: buying still arrives later and less reliably than a deckbuilder audience expects. Defense Practice is clearly beneficial, but it is a patch rather than proof that the economy is solved. The 10,000-game structured-value run moved round-one purchasing from 8.9% to 28.1%, yet 89.0% of games reached the engine's 40-round cap. The second number is partly an engine-coverage warning—unique effects are not all executable—but it also says the baseline combat numbers lack a decisive clock.

Would I play it? Yes, as a development playtest with people who enjoy tactical nonsense and will tolerate a rules lookup. I would not yet choose it over a polished published deckbuilder for a blind game night. A focused economy/clock iteration and broader effect DSL could change that.

## Best-guess BoardGameGeek-style reception

This is explicitly a design guess, not a real prediction.

Likely praise: memorable visual/voice identity, an unusually committed bureaucratic martial-arts joke, lots of card discovery, zones that make attacks and guards intuitive, and Belt/Combo goals that give players stories beyond raw victory points.

Likely complaints: a 597-card catalog feels intimidating; rules density and one-off effects invite lookup fatigue; early turns may fail to deliver the satisfying “buy every turn” deckbuilder promise; high interaction can make turns feel swingy; and humor this constant will delight some groups while exhausting others.

The split will probably be between players who enjoy Munchkin-like table chaos and collectible breadth, and players who want Dominion-like economy precision. The former may forgive balance variance if every match produces a good anecdote. The latter will demand tighter opening access, shorter games, and fewer unique exceptions.

## Not fully resolved

- Defense Practice improves but does not eliminate opening paralysis. A second economy decision is still warranted after physical playtests.
- The engine's v1 effect coverage handles structured printed values and the complete Quick Duel loop, but not every unique prose effect, Combo, Location, Belt task, tag-team rule, or Boss procedure.
- The simulation's card win associations and strategy rates are diagnostic, not balance verdicts, until that DSL coverage expands; the round-cap rate makes this especially important.
- The unrelated local `high-guard-art.webp` edit was intentionally excluded because it was not part of this audit's authored changes.
