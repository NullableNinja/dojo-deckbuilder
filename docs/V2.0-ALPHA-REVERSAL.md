# Dojo Deckbuilder v2.0 Alpha — Reversal Prototype

## Decision

**Reversal** is Paper-Fu’s clean answer to an UNO-style reverse: a fighter completely blocks an Attack, then turns that force back on its owner. It is a narrow Reaction family, not a universal “swap attacker and defender” rule.

This preserves the existing zone, Guard, Defense, Tempo, and Direct Damage rules while creating a loud, cinematic counterplay moment.

## Rules text

> **Reversal** is a Reaction played only after you play a Defense that fully Blocks an opponent’s Attack. After the blocked Attack and all of its effects resolve, you may play one Reversal.
>
> **Return X** — Deal the attacking fighter Direct Damage equal to the lower of X or that Attack’s **Incoming Damage**.
>
> **Incoming Damage** is the combat Damage the Attack would deal after its own bonuses and penalties, but before the defending fighter applies Defense, Guard, Armor, or other damage prevention or reduction.
>
> A Reversal is not an Attack and does not count as a Hit. It cannot trigger Flow, Combo effects, or a new Reaction Window. An Attack can cause only one Reversal.

### Full Block

An Attack is fully Blocked when it deals 0 combat Damage after the defender’s applicable Defense, Guard, Armor, and other prevention or reduction.

## Rules consequences

- A player must play a Defense card and fully Block the Attack; passive Character DEF or Armor alone cannot enable a Reversal.
- The original Attack was still legally played: its costs remain paid and its tags still count for card-play sequencing.
- The original Attack did not Hit. Hit-only text and Combo requirements requiring a Hit do not happen.
- Return damage is Direct Damage. It uses no ATK, Weapon, Tempo, Armor, Guard, Attack XP, or normal Defense window.
- One Attack can cause at most one Reversal. A Reversal cannot be Reversed.
- Reversal can affect a Boss after a player fully Blocks a Boss Attack. No special Boss exception is required.

## Example

Rita’s Attack has 5 Incoming Damage after her Attack card, ATK, Weapon, and Tempo are counted. Devin plays a legal Defense and fully Blocks it. After the Attack resolves, Devin plays **The Ancient Art of “No, You”** with Return 3. Rita takes 3 Direct Damage: the lower of Return 3 and the Attack’s 5 Incoming Damage.

## Balance guardrails

- Print Return values of **2–4** only in the Alpha package.
- Keep the core package to four to six Reversal cards.
- Never let Return damage open a Reaction Window or award Attack XP.
- Test first in Quick Duel and Tag Team for stall patterns, frequency, and Boss interaction.

## Deliberately rejected for core

### Literal attack redirection

Making the original Attack target its controller is funny but creates ambiguity about controller, target, attack text, equipment, and Boss behavior. It may appear on a one-off expansion card later, not as the core rule.

### Mirror every effect

Reversing all discard, Exhaust, Stun, and other penalties would create an exception factory. Those belong on individual cards, not in Reversal’s base definition.
